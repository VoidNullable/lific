A plan is a checklist of steps, nested as deeply as you need, for work that takes more than one sitting. An agent can write its plan at the start of a task, check steps off as it goes, and read the plan back in a later session to see where it stopped.

Plans are where hierarchy lives in Lific. Issues stay flat, with no sub-issues. When a piece of work breaks down into parts that deserve their own issues, the plan holds the structure and its steps point at those issues.

## Fields

| Field | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `identifier` | `APP-PLAN-2`, numbered within the project. |
| `title` | Required. |
| `status` | `active`, `done` or `archived`. New plans start `active`. See [Status](#status). |
| anchor issue | Optional. The issue this plan breaks down. See [Anchor issue](#anchor-issue). |
| steps | The tree of steps. |
| `created_at`, `updated_at` | Set by Lific. |

Plan lists show how many steps each plan has and how many are done.

## Steps

Each step has a title, an optional Markdown description, a done state, and optionally one issue it mirrors. A step can have child steps, to any depth.

A step is identified by its **step id**, a number assigned when the step is created. `get_plan` shows it as `#18`. The id never changes, even when the step moves. Its **position** is its place among its siblings, counting from 0, and it changes whenever steps are added, moved or reordered around it. Tools that change a step, such as `update_plan_step` and `edit_plan_step`, take the step id; pass `step_id: 18` for `#18`, not the step's position in the list.

You can move a step under another parent in the same plan, or to the root, and set its position among its new siblings. A step cannot move into a different plan, or beneath one of its own descendants. Deleting a step deletes every step beneath it.

`create_plan` accepts the whole tree in one call. `edit_plan_step` replaces one exact string in a step's title or description, like `edit_issue`.

## Status

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `active` | In progress. Active plans follow their issues fully; see [Mirrored issues](#mirrored-issues). |
| `done` | Finished. Its steps are no longer checked off by their issues, but an issue that reopens still unchecks its step, which reopens the plan. |
| `archived` | Put away, for instance because its anchor issue was completed. Frozen: its steps never change when their issues do. |

An active plan completes itself: when its last unchecked step is checked off, by you or by its issue, the plan moves to `done`. When a step of a `done` plan is unchecked, by you or because its issue reopened, the plan moves back to `active`. A plan with no steps never completes on its own. Both changes are written to the [activity log](/docs/concepts/comments-and-activity#activity-log) as `auto-complete` and `auto-reopen` on the plan.

You can also set the status yourself. A plan set back to `active` follows its issues again from that point on; changes made while it was `done` or `archived` are not replayed.

## Mirrored issues

A step can point at an issue, in the same project or another one. The step then mirrors that issue. The rule is that the issue's status is the source of truth: completion can flow from a step to its issue, but reopening only ever flows from the issue to its steps.

An issue is resolved when it is `done` or `cancelled`. Both check its steps off, so a plan whose dropped work was cancelled can still reach 100%. A step resolved by a cancellation is an ordinary checked step; its issue status still reads `cancelled`, which is how `get_plan` and the web UI tell it apart from finished work.

| When | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| The issue moves to `done` or `cancelled` | Every unchecked step that mirrors it, in every active plan, is checked off. |
| The issue moves between `done` and `cancelled` | Nothing happens to the steps. They stay checked. |
| The issue moves out of `done` or `cancelled` to open work | Every checked step that mirrors it, in every active or done plan, is unchecked and marked as reopened by the issue, so it does not look like someone unticked it by hand. A done plan moves back to `active`. |
| You check off a step that mirrors an open issue | The issue moves to `done`. That in turn checks off other steps mirroring it in active plans and archives plans anchored to it, as above. |
| You check off a step that mirrors a cancelled issue | Only the step changes. The issue stays `cancelled`. |
| You uncheck a step | Nothing happens to the issue. |

Steps in `archived` plans are never changed by an issue, and steps in `done` plans only by a reopen. Checking off a step in a done or archived plan still moves its issue to `done`, because that comes from your action on the step, not from the plan following its issues.

The mirror reacts to status changes, not to links. Pointing a step at an issue that is already `done` does not check the step off, and pre-marking a step done in `create_plan` does not close its issue.

Every automatic change is written to the [activity log](/docs/concepts/comments-and-activity#activity-log) as `auto-complete`, `auto-reopen` or `auto-archive`, attributed to whoever changed the issue.

## Anchor issue

A plan can name one anchor issue: the piece of work the plan breaks down, such as a feature issue whose plan lists the steps to build it. When the anchor issue moves to `done`, the plan is archived, if it is active.

This runs one way. Marking a plan `done` or `archived` never changes its anchor issue, and reopening the anchor issue does not bring an archived plan back.

## Deleted issues

When an issue a step mirrors is deleted, it goes to the trash and the link stays, so restoring the issue restores the connection. While the issue is in the trash, checking the step off changes only the step, and a plan whose anchor is in the trash shows no anchor. When the retention sweep removes the issue for good, the links to it are removed too.

## Who can change a plan

Reading a plan takes `viewer` on its project. Creating, editing and deleting plans and steps takes `maintainer`. When a step or anchor points at an issue in another project, attaching that issue, or checking off a step that would close it, also takes `maintainer` on the issue's project.

Plans are private to the project's members. [Publishing a project](/docs/public-projects) does not expose its plans.

## Deleting a plan

Deleting a plan is immediate and permanent, with all its steps. Plans have no trash. The issues its steps pointed at are not affected.
